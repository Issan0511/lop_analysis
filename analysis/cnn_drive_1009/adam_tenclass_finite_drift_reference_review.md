# Independent audit: a sparse native ten-class target and its capacity bridge

2026-10-09. Mathematical review only. No repository edits, training, or alternative activation functions were used.

**Verdict: PASS with the explicit routing/input conditions below.** At the proposed fixed state, every one of the 75 target-filter weights and its bias has a strictly positive stationary expected Adam quotient. Nonnegative dataset inputs make the actual first-filter mean field strictly positive, so the expected descent update decreases that mean. For the stated all-raw logdet capacity, the large-finite-ridge bridge also proves positive full and literal-self capacity derivatives at the same reference and on one sufficiently small full-parameter open ball.

Sections 1–7 audit the fixed-state/open-neighborhood response result. Sections 8–9 additionally audit the all-data envelope and the integrated finite-horizon transfer in `analysis/cnn_drive_1009/adam_tenclass_finite_drift.md` and its verifier. The complete result is not a theorem of indefinite moving-CNN containment or a generic ten-class sign law.

## 1. Shared-weight gradient and the class sign

Choose an interior 5x5 RGB patch of the target image whose 75 entries are all one. Suppose this is the only positive preactivation of the selected first-layer channel on the entire dataset; all other preactivations in that channel are strictly negative. Its pooled feature therefore depends locally on exactly one preactivation.

Let all downstream weights be positive on a smooth composed ReLU/MaxPool branch, and let the final positive head direction be w. Write psi=w^T h and

    kappa=partial psi/partial z_target>0.

Set ten freely trainable head rows to `a_c w`, with six coefficients a_c=-2/5 and four coefficients a_c=3/5. Set each output bias to cancel that class's logit on the target image. These are reference values, not parameter ties. Target logits are uniform and sum_c a_c=0.

For the six-class indicator I, under a uniform ten-class target label,

    xi=3/5-I, I~Bernoulli(3/5),
    a_Y=xi.

Every target-filter weight sees input derivative one at that patch, and the bias also has derivative one. Therefore all 76 independent raw coordinates have the identical batch gradient

    g_t=-(kappa/16) I{target image belongs to batch_t} xi_task.  (1.1)

All other images have zero derivative with respect to this filter because the entire selected channel is locally inactive on them. Their labels can be genuinely independent and arbitrary; they do not enter (1.1). This requires a samplewise CNN/mean loss, as in the stated native Conv/ReLU/MaxPool/FC architecture; a batch-coupling layer would require a separate argument.

Sharing the filter does not invalidate (1.1). It is exactly why the update generated by the single active patch also changes preactivations at all inactive positions. Equality of the 76 gradients is a property of the patch and routing, not a constraint tying the 76 trainable parameters.

## 2. Retained moments, task reuse, and the exponent 149

Condition on the complete sequence of epoch permutations. For the positive-gradient counterpart `+(kappa/16) I_target xi_task`, group the first- and second-EMA weights of target visits by task:

    A_k=sum_(target visits in task k) (1-beta1) beta1^lag,
    B_k=sum_(target visits in task k) (1-beta2) beta2^lag.

Then A_k,B_k>=0 and their sums are at most one. The independent variables are the target labels of different tasks. Repeated visits during the 400 epochs of one task use the same xi and must remain grouped.

The exact stationary quotient of this positive counterpart is

    Q=[sum_k A_k xi_k]/[sqrt(sum_k B_k xi_k^2)+e],
    e=16 epsilon/kappa.

For q=3/5, conditioning on every xi except xi_k gives a nonpositive term because the larger positive value q has the larger squared denominator contribution. The quantitative two-point lemma yields

    E[Q|schedule] <= -c(q,e) sum_k A_k B_k,
    c(q,e)=(1-q)(2q-1)/[2(q+e)^2]>0.               (2.1)

The actual gradients (1.1) negate the entire numerator history and preserve the second moment, so their expected quotients have the opposite, positive sign.

There is one target visit in every 75-update epoch. The gap between successive visits is at most 149 updates. At any stationary optimizer phase the newest target visit therefore has lag at most 149; this is a conservative endpoint convention. It holds across task boundaries as well as within tasks. If that visit lies in task k, its individual contribution gives

    A_k B_k >=(1-beta1)(1-beta2)(beta1 beta2)^149.

Grouping other visits with the same reused label only adds nonnegative cross-products. Thus at the standard betas every target raw coordinate obeys

    E[q_actual,j]
       >=c(3/5,16epsilon/kappa)
          (1/10)(1/1000)(8991/10000)^149>0.         (2.2)

The proof uses no independence between successive batches, no moment reset, and no independent redraw of the target label during an epoch or task. The infinite past is essential to the stationary statement; a finite-initialized process before its first target visit is not asserted to satisfy (2.2).

## 3. The actual filter mean

For fixed images, the true spatial/data mean is affine in the target raw filter block:

    m(theta)=sum_(j=1)^75 mu_patch,j w_j+b,
    u=(mu_patch,1).

If all images are nonnegative and padding is zero, u_j>=0 and u_bias=1. Since the 76 stationary quotient expectations coincide, writing their common positive value as Qbar gives

    u^T Fbar_target=(1+sum_j mu_patch,j)Qbar>0.      (3.1)

For a phase-summed field, sum the positive phase values. Consequently the averaged Adam descent changes this actual mean in the negative direction.

The target patch being all ones alone would not establish (3.1) for arbitrary negative-valued input datasets. Nonnegative images, or the explicit condition `1+sum_j mu_patch,j>0`, is needed. The proposed images in [0,1] satisfy it.

## 4. Native realizability and MaxPool qualifications

The concrete first-layer choice is sound:

    all target weights=1, target bias=-74.5;
    one interior 5x5 RGB all-ones block in the target image;
    every other pixel of that image lies in (0,.1);
    all other 1199 distinct images lie in (0,.1).

The matching convolution patch has preactivation 75-74.5=.5. Any different 5x5 patch intersects the block in at most 60 scalar entries, and its other 15 entries are below .1. Its input sum is below 61.5, so its preactivation is below -13. Other-image sums are below 7.5, giving preactivation below -67. These are strict, finite margins. The filter is already strongly negative in its bulk mean; this construction does not show a positive-mean filter crossing zero.

The positive target wins its first-pool window uniquely. Every other first-pool window of this channel consists of locally identically zero ReLU outputs. These unavoidable ties are harmless: the composed parameter-to-feature function is locally the constant zero function, independently of the library's argmax choice.

At the second convolution, the target's influence must reach a selected pool output so that kappa>0. A center-dominant positive kernel plus appropriate winner margins is one sufficient construction. More generally, an interior target produces a 5x5 positive-influence footprint through positive second-layer kernels; a fully covered 2x2 pooling window has positive target derivative whichever of its entries wins. Positive FC paths then carry a nonzero derivative to psi.

Genuine competing MaxPool branches whose values merely coincide at the reference should be separated by a strict perturbation. Generic positive kernel/background choices can do this while preserving the first-layer margins. Structural ties of locally identical smooth functions are different and are permitted. In particular, in the literal-self network on a nontarget image, zero first-layer features and a positive second-layer bias make every second-layer site exactly the same positive function b2. Those ties are smooth too. The permissible class is therefore **locally identical smooth branches, including zero branches**, not only identically-zero ties.

The target patch must be interior so that its 75 augmented input entries are genuinely one rather than padded zeros. The input and routing construction leaves all raw weights and biases independently free; small independent perturbations preserve the strict composed branch.

## 5. Exact all-raw NTK and positive trace derivative

Let H be the N-by-d final hidden feature matrix and define

    psi_n=w^T h_n,
    D_(n,j)=partial_(hidden raw j) psi_n.

The hidden coordinates in D exclude the free output-head weights and output biases, which are counted separately. At the class-rank-one reference `V_c=a_c w`, the complete raw logit NTK is

    K=(HH^T+11^T) tensor I_10+(DD^T) tensor aa^T.  (5.1)

Let `H_u=D_u H` and `E_u=D_u D`, where u is supported on the target first-filter block. Then

    K'=(H_u H^T+H H_u^T) tensor I_10
        +(E_u D^T+D E_u^T) tensor aa^T.            (5.2)

The negative target bias does not justify a blanket nonnegative-polynomial-coefficient claim. Instead use the actual nonnegative activations and positive downstream path weights on the fixed composed branch. They give D>=0 and H_u>=0. The latter is zero on every nontarget image and nonzero on the target. Mixed differentiation in the first-filter direction gives E_u>=0: first-layer/bias gradient coefficients are constant in that direction, while downstream weight gradients differentiate nonnegative upstream activations in a nonnegative direction. Thus

    S:=tr K'
       =20<H,H_u>+2||a||^2<D,E_u>>0.              (5.3)

Here `||a||^2=12/5`. Strictness already follows from the free-head feature term; no positive definiteness of the whole K' is asserted. Cross-image terms can make K' indefinite.

The Schatten/nuclear bound is

    ||K'||_* <= T,
    T=20||H||_F||H_u||_F
       +2||a||^2||D||_F||E_u||_F.                 (5.4)

It follows from `||AB^T||_*<=||A||_F||B||_F`, the triangle inequality, and the tensor nuclear norms of I_10 and aa^T. All retained biases are included.

The same argument applies independently to the literal channel-isolated architecture, with its retained positive downstream weights/biases and its own H,D. Its output bias need not cancel its target logits: the NTK and capacity calculation do not require that CE equilibrium. The stationary mean-direction proof concerns the full network.

## 6. Finite-ridge capacity certificate and an open intersection

For the exact functional

    C_lambda(theta)=.5 logdet(I+K(theta)/lambda), lambda>0,

its derivative is `.5 tr[(lambda I+K)^(-1)K']`. Put R=tr K. Since K is positive semidefinite,

    ||(lambda I+K)^(-1)-lambda^(-1)I||_op
       <=||K||_op/lambda^2<=R/lambda^2.

Combining with (5.3)-(5.4) gives

    D_u C_lambda >=S/(2lambda)-R T/(2lambda^2).     (6.1)

Hence any finite `lambda>R T/S` certifies strict positivity. Choose one common lambda above both the full and literal-self thresholds. This is a large-finite-ridge existence certificate, not a claim for an arbitrary prescribed small ridge or a differently defined capacity functional.

At the resulting finite reference, the stationary mean-field margin is strict, and the stationary quotient is continuous under uniformly small gradient-history perturbations by the RMS norm inequality and epsilon>0. The full/self capacity derivatives are also continuous on the smooth composed routing neighborhood. A sufficiently small open ball of the **entire raw parameter space** therefore preserves the same mean-field sign and both original logdet-capacity signs at that common lambda. The ball need not preserve exact class grouping, equal first-filter weights, or exactly uniform target logits; those equalities were only used to derive the reference margin.

## 7. Limits of the audited lemma

The target filter has one effective training image and is locally inactive on the other 1199. Other channels may remain active and overlapping, and all labels remain iid uniform, but this is not a balanced general-purpose feature family. The already negative bulk mean and the chosen skew class-head orientation are substantive construction choices.

The standalone lemma concerns the stationary expected response of a frozen CNN and an open local family of such states. It does not assert that current trained-state histories are independent of old labels, that every realized update points downward, or that the model remains in this open ball forever. The separately proved constant-rate compact-exit obstruction still applies to the complete raw network. A transfer to a moving hidden observable or a finite training horizon requires the additional arguments audited in section 9 below.

## 8. All-data positive envelope and verifier audit

The proposed envelope is valid. Use an all-ones image with the same zero-padding convention, retain all positive weights and biases, replace the negative first target bias by its absolute value, and replace each 2x2 MaxPool by the sum of its four inputs. All envelope preactivations are positive. The envelope is used only as an auxiliary bound; it does not change the trained architecture or activation.

Forward induction bounds every original nonnegative hidden activation by the corresponding envelope activation. The input comparison is coordinatewise, the affine weights are nonnegative, the absolute bias dominates the original bias, and a maximum of nonnegative entries is bounded by their sum.

For raw derivatives, backward induction is equally important. The derivative of any fixed MaxPool route is an entrywise nonnegative selector bounded by the derivative of the four-entry sum. ReLU derivatives on the smooth composite branch are either zero or one. The remaining backward maps use the same nonnegative weights. Starting from the positive vector w, every original adjoint is bounded coordinatewise by the envelope adjoint. A shared-convolution raw-weight derivative is a sum of products of an upstream activation and a downstream adjoint, so both factors are bounded and nonnegative. Bias derivatives are sums of adjoints. Consequently every hidden raw derivative satisfies

    0<=h_n<=h_env, 0<=D_n<=D_env,
    ||H||_F<=sqrt(N)||h_env||_2,
    ||D||_F<=sqrt(N)||D_env||_2.

This comparison includes independently trainable first-layer coordinates. The envelope derivative is with respect to its already-positive bias value: it is not the derivative of the map that takes the original negative bias through abs. The implementation correctly forms the new positive envelope parameter vector and differentiates the positive network at that vector.

Since H_u and E_u vanish on all nontarget images, the target-only derivatives suffice for their all-data Frobenius norms and for S. With `h_bound=sqrt(N)||h_env||` and `d_bound=sqrt(N)||D_env||`, the verifier uses

    R_+=10(h_bound^2+N)+||a||^2 d_bound^2,
    T_+=20 h_bound ||H_u||+2||a||^2 d_bound ||E_u||.

The N term is the raw output-bias contribution and is correctly retained. The separate choice `lambda=2 R_+ T_+/S` is strictly above the sufficient threshold. Taking the larger of the full and self choices preserves strict positivity for both. No full 12000-by-12000 NTK formation is needed for this bound.

Read-only code inspection found the native target-block indices, literal-self coordinate restriction, image-major/class-minor tensor ordering, shared-weight derivatives, and common-ridge calculation consistent with the formulas. The exact Fraction stationary lower bound is explicitly conditional on kappa>=1/1000; the native kappa and capacities are checked in float64. These are finite numerical identity checks and analytic-envelope evaluations, not outward-rounded interval certificates. The native numeric checks on three images are not themselves an all-data NTK verification; the all-data conclusion uses the analytic envelope and inactive-gate construction. No test or training run was repeated for this audit.

## 9. Integrated finite constant-rate transfer

The integrated theorem's section 5 correctly supplies the additional finite-horizon argument. A closed smooth-branch ball on which the independently established margins persist is selected first. Its existence does not presume future trajectory containment. The universal globally bias-corrected Adam bound `||q_t||_infinity<=73` gives a deterministic displacement bound for every label/shuffle history.

Writing M=73H, initial distance<=r/4, `0<S<r/(4M)`, `0<eta<S`, and `K=ceil(S/eta)` gives

    M K eta <=M(S+eta)<2MS<r/2.

The initial offset therefore keeps every intermediate update strictly within distance 3r/4 of the reference. Applying this bound up to a hypothetical first exit rules out that exit. The auxiliary cutoff process and the original process consequently coincide on this entire finite interval. This uses no infinite-time stability assumption.

For the linear observable m, the existing whole-task observable-averaging theorem yields the displayed signed cumulative remainder. The stated terms account for initialization/global bias correction, drifting within-task parameters, the Poisson corrector, and its martingale maximal bound; the Hessian/Taylor term is zero. At fixed S and alpha, `K eta` stays bounded, `K eta^2` and `eta sqrt(K)` vanish, and the corrector modulus tends to zero. Thus there is a strictly positive constant eta with remainder at most dS/2. On the probability event the task-summed lower drift d gives

    m(theta_K)-m(theta_0)<=-d K eta+dS/2<=-dS/2.

The optional unconditional-expectation statement also checks: on failure the absolute displacement is below 2UMS, so `alpha<d/(d+4UM)` makes the expected terminal displacement strictly negative.

The quantifiers are essential. Eta is selected after the local geometry, desired small clock horizon S, and failure probability. Making eta smaller increases K while keeping this short clock horizon fixed. This does not prove a prescribed practical eta, arbitrarily long time at one fixed eta, an arbitrary prescribed ridge, or convergence of the full raw network. Subject to those explicit restrictions, the integrated finite theorem and the envelope argument pass this independent audit.

## 10. Alternative analytic reference with strictly positive initial mean

**Verdict: PASS.** This additional construction removes the already-negative initial mean of section 4. It is an analytic alternative, not a claim that the previously executed native float64 dataset checks used these new images or parameters. No new training or native numerical verification was performed.

Take

    N=1200, L=32^2, T=N L=1228800,
    b0=1/10, A=9/10, tau=A/(4T)=3/16384000.

Set the target first filter's red center coefficient to one, its other 74 coefficients to zero, and its bias to -b0. Put every RGB background pixel strictly in `(b0-2tau,b0-tau)`. In one target image replace one interior red pixel by `b0+A=1`. The background values can vary within the interval to make all 1200 images distinct and to make nonidentical pool competitors generic.

The word center is important: at every first-layer spatial site this filter reads the actual red pixel at that site. Its only nonzero coefficient never lands on padding. Thus exactly one preactivation is A and all other T-1 preactivations lie in `(-2tau,-tau)`. The exceptional spatial site must be at least two pixels from the image edge so that all 75 coordinates of its complete winning patch are actual positive pixels. The unused off-center coefficients can therefore acquire both positive and negative small perturbations without destroying the strict gate pattern; they are not frozen.

The actual mean at the reference is

    m*= [A+sum_(other T-1 sites)(red_pixel-b0)]/T
       > A/T-2tau(T-1)/T
       > A/T-2tau=A/(2T)=3/8192000>0.          (10.1)

Here `3/8192000` is approximately `3.662109375e-7`. The strict inactive-gate margin is at least tau, approximately `1.8310546875e-7`. These margins are small but nonzero. The minimum target-patch coefficient satisfies

    c_j=x_target_patch,j > c_min=b0-2tau
       =819197/8192000>0,
    c_bias=1.

The same positive downstream path construction gives kappa>0, and the same six/four head values and free bias cancellation make target logits uniform. The exact target-block gradient streams are now

    g_(t,j)=-(kappa c_j/16) d_t xi_task.         (10.2)

They have different positive scales, which is harmless. Applying the grouped two-point lemma to each coordinate gives

    E q_j^*
       >=c(3/5,16epsilon/(kappa c_j)) omega_*
       >=c(3/5,16epsilon/(kappa c_min)) omega_*>0,

where `omega_*=(1-beta1)(1-beta2)(beta1 beta2)^149`. The last inequality uses that c(q,e) decreases with e. No equality between raw-coordinate gradients is needed. Since u is the nonnegative actual augmented patch average and its bias entry is one, its stationary mean projection is again strictly positive at every phase, and the task-summed margin is at least H times this common lower bound times `U=sum u_j`.

The full/literal-self capacity argument also survives. First-layer coefficients need not all be strictly positive: all forward activations are nonnegative, the downstream weights and w are positive, and the composed gates and routes remain fixed. First-layer raw sensitivities depend on the inputs and downstream paths, not on the value of their own coefficient. Their u derivatives vanish. Downstream raw-weight sensitivities have nonnegative u derivatives through their upstream activations. Consequently D,H_u,E_u remain nonnegative at the reference, with a nonzero target H_u, and the same finite-ridge trace/nuclear bound applies. The auxiliary envelope remains valid at this reference because all first-layer weights are nonnegative, including the zero entries. Exact zero coefficients and head factorization are only reference choices; continuity supplies a full raw open ball with the strict mean-field/capacity signs.

The finite trajectory can be kept at a positive mean pathwise, independently of the probability event for net descent. In addition to the smooth-branch containment requirements of section 9, choose its ball radius r and clock horizon S so that

    U r < m*/2,
    S < m*/(8 U M), M=73H,
    0<eta<S, K=ceil(S/eta).

The theorem's initial ball of radius r/4 in particular has `m(theta_0)>m*/2`; even radius r would suffice for this mean estimate. At every update through task K,

    |m(theta_t)-m(theta_0)| <= U M K eta
       <2 U M S <m*/4.

Therefore `m(theta_t)>m*/4>0` throughout the finite run for every label/shuffle history. Choosing eta still smaller to meet the averaging remainder budget simultaneously gives, with probability at least 1-alpha,

    0<m(theta_K)<=m(theta_0)-dS/2<m(theta_0).

Thus the refinement proves **positive mean to lower positive mean**, while retaining one active target site and inactive other sites. It does not prove crossing zero, death, a widely active feature, a practical numerical rate, or unlimited time. The strict support neighborhood also keeps the target active, so its survival is part of this finite construction rather than an inferred general behavior of ReLU.

The displayed rational constants were checked with Python's exact Fraction arithmetic only: tau, c_min, the lower mean bound, `b0+A=1`, and the strict algebraic improvement `A-2tau(T-1)>T[A/(2T)]` agree. This check does not certify a native floating-point implementation or a learning-rate threshold.
